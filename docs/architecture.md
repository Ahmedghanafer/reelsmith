# Architecture

The page goes from the data to the screen: the Core package, the Engine, the app with its screens, and the word files. The tests are described in `docs/testing.md`.

## The Core package

`Sources/ReelsmithCore` imports nothing but the standard library and Foundation, and is tested on Linux. The types in the files below are public values, Equatable and Sendable, but `TimelineGeometry`, which holds only constants and functions.

### The model

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Model/Time.swift`: `Time`, a whole number of ticks, 600 a second.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Model/Media.swift`: `MediaID` and `MediaInfo`, the facts of one media file.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Model/Canvas.swift`: A project has one of two shapes, and the plan and the Engine follow it. `Canvas` is an enum with the two cases `landscape` (16:9, 1920 by 1080 pixels) and `portrait` (9:16, 1080 by 1920 pixels), in that order in `allCases`. `width` and `height` are its pixels. `words` gives the word key of a case, `start.landscape` or `start.portrait`, and `allWords` lists the two. `startChoice` is `landscape`, the shape the start screen has chosen before she taps.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Model/Project.swift`: `ClipID`, `Clip` and `Project`. Clips always touch, so every place is computed from the clip lengths. The canvas on the project: `Project.canvas`. `Project()` is `portrait` and `Project(canvas:)` takes the other one. `Project` has no size of its own, only `framesPerSecond`.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Model/Words.swift`: `Words`, a word key with its arguments. Core never holds a word a person reads.

### Text in the model

A text is a value of the project. All its geometry is in canvas pixels from the top left of the canvas, a rotation is in degrees clockwise, and a time is a `Time`.

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Model/Text.swift`: `TextID`. `TextFont`, the three faces `naskh`, `naskhBold` and `system` in that order in `allCases`, with the raw values "naskh", "naskh-bold" and "system". `words` gives the word key of a face, `text.font.naskh`, `text.font.naskhBold` or `text.font.system`, and `allWords` lists the three. `TextAlign`, a side of the picture: `left`, `centre` or `right`. `TextStyle`, the face, the size, the colour, the side, the line spacing as a share of the size, and the three switches `outline`, `shadow` and `box`. `TextStyle.standard` is `naskh` at size 64, white, centred, with no line spacing and the three switches off. `TextStyle.palette` holds eight colours, white first. `TextRange` is a start and a length. `TextSpan` is `whole`, from zero to the end of the project whatever its length becomes, or a range. `TextItem` is one text: its id, its string as typed with "\n" for a line break, its span, the centre of its block as `x` and `y`, its rotation and its style. `isBlank` is true when the string is empty or holds only spaces and line breaks.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Model/Project.swift`: `Project.texts` is the list of texts in drawing order, the later one on top, and `nextTextID` starts at 1. A new project has no text. `text(_:)` gives the text of an id and `position(of:)` its place in the list, 1 for the first, each nil for an unknown id. `range(of:)` gives the range of a text as it is seen: zero to the project length for a `whole` text, and a range cut at the project end. It is nil for an unknown id, for an empty project, and for a range that starts at or after the end. `sameFootage(as:)` is true when canvas, clips, media and the three values of sound of two projects are equal, whatever their texts and the framing of their clips.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/TextAction.swift`: `TextAction`, the eight things a screen can ask for a text: `add`, `select`, `setString`, `setStyle`, `move`, `turn`, `trimStart` and `trimEnd`. `EditorAction` carries one as the case `text(TextAction)`.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/TextEdits.swift`: the two functions `reduce` calls for text. `reduceText` gets the state and the text action of the case `text` and returns the state after it. `settleTexts(from:to:)` gets the state before and after every action, on every path of `reduce`, and returns the state after with every blank text removed that is not the selected one. What each action does stands under "Text edits" below.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/TextGeometry.swift`: `TextGeometry.wrapShare`, 0.8, the share of the canvas width at which a line wraps, the limits `minimumSize`, `maximumSize` and `minimumText`, and `canvasLength(ofPoints:pictureWidth:canvas:)`.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/EditorState.swift`: `selectedText` is the id of the selected text or nil, and nil in a new state. At most one of `selection`, `selectedText` and `showsSound` is set: `select` of a clip of the project sets `selectedText` to nil, and `clearSelection` sets both to nil and `showsSound` to false. A `select` of a clip id that is not in the project changes nothing. `canAddText` is true when the project has a clip. `textInfo` is "none" when `selectedText` is nil or names no text of the project. Else it is ten fields with one space between them: the position of the text, the raw value of its font, its size, its colour as "r,g,b", the raw value of its side, its centre as "x,y", its rotation, three marks for outline, shadow and box ("o", "s", "b", or "." for off), its range as the start in ticks, a plus sign and the length in ticks, or "hidden" when it has no range, and its line spacing times 100. Size, centre, rotation and line spacing are rounded to whole numbers, and every digit is one of 0 to 9. The string of the text is not a field. The text of the seed `text-wide` reads `1 naskh 64 255,255,255 centre 960,540 0 ... 0+4200 0`.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Plan/RenderPlan.swift`: `RenderPlan.texts` is the list of `TextLayer` values of a plan, in the order of `Project.texts`. `renderPlan(for:)` leaves out a blank text and a text with no range, so an empty project gives no layer. A layer holds the id, the string, `timelineStart` and `duration` from `range(of:)`, the centre, the rotation, the style, and `lineWidth`, the plan's width times `wrapShare`: 1536 on the landscape canvas and 864 on the portrait one at `standard`. A layer is drawn in a frame at time t when `timelineStart <= t < timelineStart + duration`. A plan built with no texts has none.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Seeds/Seed.swift`: the two seeds with a text and `Seed.selectedText`, described under the seeds below.
- `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/TextPicture.swift`: `TextPicture`, an image with its width and height in pixels of the plan's picture, and the internal `textPicture(for:)`, which takes a `TextLayer` and draws it, as "Faces and the picture of a text" says. Nothing calls it, so no text is drawn in the preview or in an exported file.
- The three word files hold five groups for the words of text: `add-text`, with the six keys of the text button and the text panel, and `text-look`, `text-more`, `text-row` and `text-path`, which hold no key.

### Text edits

What each text action does, in `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/TextEdits.swift`. A value past a limit is held at the limit and never refused. An action that names a text that is not in the project returns the state unchanged, the playing flag included. So does a `setStyle` with a size or a line spacing, a `move` with an x or a y, and a `turn` with an angle that is not a finite number: not a number, or infinite.

- `add` appends one text and selects it: the next text id, an empty string, `whole`, the centre of the canvas (960, 540 on the landscape canvas and 540, 960 on the portrait one), rotation 0 and `TextStyle.standard`. `nextTextID` grows by 1, so an id is never used twice. `selection` becomes nil and the state is paused. On a project with no clip it changes nothing.
- `select` sets `selectedText`, sets `selection` to nil and leaves `isPlaying` as it was.
- `setString` stores the string as given.
- `setStyle` stores the style with its size held between `TextGeometry.minimumSize`, 16, and `maximumSize`, 400, and its line spacing held between 0 and 2. Face, colour, side and the three switches are stored as given.
- `move` stores the new centre held inside the canvas, and the edge is inside: x from 0 to the canvas width and y from 0 to the canvas height, so 0 to 1920 and 0 to 1080 on the landscape canvas.
- `turn` stores an angle above minus 180 and at most 180: whole turns are taken off, so 190 becomes minus 170 and minus 180 becomes 180. An angle at most 3 from 0, 3 included, becomes 0.
- `trimStart` and `trimEnd` work on `range(of:)`, the range as it is seen, and a positive amount makes the text shorter. The amount is held so that the range stays inside the project, from zero to the project length, and stays at least `TextGeometry.minimumText` long, 60 ticks, and is then rounded toward zero to whole frames. The result is stored as a range, or as `whole` when it runs from zero to the project length. A trim of a text with no range changes nothing. When the seen range is shorter than `minimumText`, because a clip edit cut it, a `trimStart` moves the start back until the range is at least `minimumText` long, and a `trimEnd` changes nothing.
- `setString`, `setStyle`, `move`, `turn` and a trim that is done pause the state, also when the value is the one the text had.
- The blank rule, `settleTexts(from:to:)`: a blank text exists only while it is selected. After every action, on every path of `reduce`, each blank text that is not the selected one is removed from the project. So `clearSelection`, a `select` of a clip, a `select` of another text and an `add` all remove a blank text that was selected, and a `setString` that gives a blank string to a text that is not selected removes that text.
- `deleteSelected`, in `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/Edits.swift`: when `selectedText` is set it removes that text, sets `selectedText` to nil, pauses and touches no clip. `canDelete` is true when `selectedText` names a text of the project, and with no selected text when `selection` names a clip.
- No clip action changes a text. A text whose range lies past the end of a shortened project keeps its values, has no range and no layer in the plan, and has its range again when the project grows.
- `Project.text(nearestToX:y:at:)`, in the same file as the text actions: among the texts that are not blank and whose range holds the time (the start is inside, the end is not), the id of the one whose centre is nearest to the place in canvas pixels, the later of the list on a tie, and nil when there is none.
- `TextGeometry.canvasLength(ofPoints:pictureWidth:canvas:)`, in `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/TextGeometry.swift`: a length in points of the screen as canvas pixels, the points times the canvas width divided by the width of the picture in points, signed, and 0 when that width is not above 0.

The text actions keep a valid state valid: every text id is used once, every stored range starts at a whole frame not below zero and is a whole number of frames of at least `minimumText`, every size lies between 16 and 400, every centre lies inside the canvas, every rotation is above minus 180 and at most 180, `selectedText` is a text of the project or nil, at most one selection is set, and a blank text exists only while it is selected.

### Sound in the model

Sound is three values of the project: one volume and one mute for the sound of all clips, and at most one music. Each clip also has a volume of its own, under "Photos and the volume of a clip". A volume is a number from 0 to 1, the volume of a clip from 0 to 2, and a plain factor, and a time is a `Time` in whole frames.

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Model/Sound.swift`: `MusicFile`, the facts of one music file: its id, its file name inside the media folder, its title, which is content and stored as given, and its length. `Music` is a file with the place in the file where the music begins (`start`), a volume and the two fades `fadeIn` and `fadeOut`. `Music(file:)` begins at zero at the volume 1 with no fade. `largestStart` is the length of the file less `SoundGeometry.minimumMusic`, rounded down to a whole frame, and zero for a file not longer than that. `SoundGeometry` holds three constants: `maximumFade`, 3000 ticks, `minimumMusic`, 600 ticks, which a start leaves of the file at least, and `startStep`, 600 ticks, the step of a control that sets the start.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Model/Project.swift`: `clipVolume` is 1, `clipsMuted` is false and `music` is nil in a new project. The music always begins at the start of the video. `heardMusic` is how long the music sounds: the smaller of what is left of the file after its start and the project length. It is nil with no music and for a project with no clip, which keeps its music and hears nothing of it. No clip action and no text action changes one of the three values, so the music follows every trim through `heardMusic` alone. `sameFootage(as:)` compares the three values too, so a change of sound builds a new player item, as "The preview" says.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/SoundAction.swift`: `SoundAction`, the ten things a screen can ask for sound: `open`, `setClipVolume`, `muteClips`, `setMusic`, `removeMusic`, `setMusicVolume`, `setMusicStart`, `setFadeIn`, `setFadeOut` and `setVolumeOfClip`, the last under "Photos and the volume of a clip". `EditorAction` carries one as the case `sound(SoundAction)`.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/SoundEdits.swift`: the two functions `reduce` calls for sound. `reduceSound` gets the state and the sound action of the case `sound`, and `settleSound` gets the state after every action, on every path of `reduce`, last. What they do is under "Sound edits".
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/EditorState.swift`: `showsSound` is the sign that the sound panel shows, false in a new state, and `clearSelection` sets it to false. `canOpenSound` is true when the project has a clip. `canSetMusicStart` is true when there is music and its `largestStart` is at least `startStep`: true for a file of 1200 ticks, false for one of 900. `soundInfo` is three fields with one space between them when there is no music: the volume of the clips times 100 rounded to a whole number, "m" for muted or "." for not, and "none". With music it is six: the same two, then the start in ticks, a plus sign and the heard length in ticks, or "hidden" when `heardMusic` is nil, the volume of the music times 100, and the fade in and the fade out in ticks, both as stored. The seed `two-clips-wide` reads `100 . none` and the seed `music-wide` reads `100 . 0+4200 100 0 0`. `musicLabel` gives the part of the file that is heard and the length of the file as clock texts, as in "00:00-00:07 / 00:10", and is nil when `heardMusic` is nil.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Plan/RenderPlan.swift`: `RenderPlan.sound` is a `SoundPlan`: `clipVolume`, the volume of the clips as it is heard, which is 0 when they are muted, and `music`, a `MusicLayer` or nil. `renderPlan(for:)` gives a layer when `heardMusic` is not nil: the id and the file name of the file, `sourceStart`, the start in the file, `duration`, the heard length, for which the music sounds on the timeline from zero, the volume, and the two fades, each cut to half of `duration`. So fades of 3000 and 3000 ticks on 4200 heard ticks are 2100 and 2100 in the layer, and the stored values stay. `SoundPlan.plain` is the volume 1 with no music, and a plan built with no sound has it.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Seeds/TestTone.swift`: `TestTone`, the values of the two test tones, each with a pitch for the first half of the file and one for the second, a peak of 0.25 of full scale, 44100 samples a second and 1 channel. `long-tone` is 6000 ticks, 880 Hz then 1320 Hz. `short-tone` is 1200 ticks at 660 Hz. `all` is long then short. `file` gives the `MusicFile` of a tone: the id and the title are its name, and the file name is the name with ".mov". No file is written here.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Seeds/Seed.swift`: the seed `music-wide` (`Seed.musicWide`) is `two-clips-wide` with `Music(file: TestTone.long.file)`, so 4200 ticks of the long tone are heard. `Seed.testTones` is the long tone for this seed and empty for the other seven. `Seed.showsSound` is true for this seed alone. The seed start of the app writes the tones and opens the sound panel from the two, as "The sound panel" says.
- The three word files end with six groups for the words of sound, `sound-panel`, `sound-clips`, `add-music`, `music-levels`, `music-video` and `sound-path`. The group `sound-panel` holds the five keys of "The sound panel". Each other group holds the keys of the part that fills it, named under its heading, and a group whose part is not built yet holds no key.

### Sound edits

What each sound action does, in `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/SoundEdits.swift`. A value past a limit is held at the limit and never refused. An action that would change nothing returns the state unchanged, the playing flag included. So does a volume that is not a finite number and, for the four actions on the music other than `setMusic`, an action on a project with no music. An action that changes only a level (`setClipVolume`, `muteClips`, `setVolumeOfClip`, `setMusicVolume`, `setFadeIn` and `setFadeOut`) leaves `isPlaying` as it was. Every other action that changes the state pauses it, but `open`.

- `open` sets `showsSound` to true and both `selection` and `selectedText` to nil, and leaves `isPlaying` as it was. A blank text that was selected goes by the blank rule. On a project with no clip it changes nothing.
- `setClipVolume` stores the volume of the clips held between 0 and 1.
- `muteClips` stores the mute. The volume of the clips stays, and the plan reads 0 while they are muted.
- `setMusic` replaces any music by `Music(file:)`: start zero, volume 1 and no fade, so nothing of the music before is kept. A file whose length is not above zero changes nothing. Music may be set on a project with no clip, where nothing of it is heard.
- `removeMusic` sets `music` to nil and leaves the volume and the mute of the clips.
- `setMusicVolume` stores the volume of the music held between 0 and 1.
- `setMusicStart` stores the start held between zero and `Music.largestStart` and rounded toward zero to a whole frame: 619 ticks become 600, and on `long-tone`, 6000 ticks, the largest start is 5400, which leaves 600 heard. On `short-tone`, 1200 ticks, it is 600, and on a file of 600 ticks or less it is 0.
- `setFadeIn` and `setFadeOut` store the fade held between zero and `SoundGeometry.maximumFade`, 3000 ticks, and rounded toward zero to a whole frame. A fade longer than half of what is heard is stored as sent and cut only in the plan.
- Every action but `open` works whether the panel shows or not and leaves `showsSound` alone.
- `settleSound`, the one of three: after every action, on every path of `reduce`, `showsSound` is set to false when a clip or a text is selected and when the project has no clip. So a `select` of a clip, a `select` of a text, an `add` of a text and the delete of the last clip close the panel, and `clearSelection` closes it in `reduce` itself. With the panel open and nothing selected, `canDelete` is false: the music is removed by `removeMusic` alone.
- No clip action and no text action changes the volume of the clips, the mute or the music.

The sound actions keep a valid state valid: the volume of the clips and of the music lie from 0 to 1, the start of the music is a whole frame from zero to the largest start, both fades are whole frames from zero to 3000 ticks, the file of the music is longer than zero, at most one of `selection`, `selectedText` and `showsSound` is set, and `showsSound` is false on a project with no clip.

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Start/MusicImport.swift`: `MusicImport`, the state the music part draws while a file comes in, with the cases `idle`, `copying` and `failed(isProtected:)`. `after(_:)` moves between them on a `MusicImport.Event`, in five moves: `picked` makes `idle` and `failed` into `copying`, `imported` makes `copying` into `idle`, `refused(isProtected:)` makes `copying` into `failed` with the same flag, and `failureConfirmed` makes `failed` into `idle`. Every other pair leaves the case unchanged, so a pick while copying changes nothing and one file comes in at a time. `words` is nil for `idle`, then `Words("music.copying")`, and `Words("music.notReadable")` or `Words("music.protected")` for a refusal. `allWords` lists those three keys.

### Photos and the volume of a clip

A photo is a clip whose media has the kind `photo`, and every clip has a volume of its own. Nothing in Core treats a photo apart but `addClips` and the plan.

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Model/Media.swift`: `MediaKind`, `video` or `photo`, and `MediaInfo.kind`, last in its members and in its initialiser, where it is `video` unless given. The media facts of a photo have its upright size, `quarterTurns` 0, `hasSound` false and the duration `MediaInfo.photoLongest`, 108000 ticks (3 minutes). `MediaInfo.photoLength` is 1800 ticks (3 seconds).
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Model/Project.swift`: `Clip.volume`, from 0 to 2, a plain factor on the volume of the clips, last in its members and in its initialiser, where it is 1 unless given. `clipInfo(_:)` gives one clip as "<media id> <sourceStart ticks>+<duration ticks> <volume times 100, rounded> <v or p>", as "blue 0+1800 100 v" and "magenta 0+1800 100 p", with "v" for a video and "p" for a photo, and nil for an unknown id.
- The two new members are stored. Each type reads them in an `init(from:)` of its own file with `decodeIfPresent`, so a draft written before them reads as a video and a volume of 1. The coding keys and the encoding stay synthesized and `Draft.version` stays 1.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/Edits.swift`: `addClips` gives a photo the length `photoLength` from the start of its media, and every other media its whole length as before. The trims change a photo clip as any clip, up to `photoLongest`, and a `trimStart` before zero changes nothing. `split` gives both halves the volume of the clip, and a delete leaves the volume of the clips that stay.
- `.sound(.setVolumeOfClip(id, volume))`, in `SoundEdits.swift`: stores the volume of that clip held between 0 and 2, and leaves `isPlaying` as it was, as a level does. An unknown id, the value the clip has and a value that is not finite return the state unchanged, the playing flag included. No other action changes the volume of a clip.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Plan/RenderPlan.swift`: `Segment.isStill`, true when the media is a photo, and `Segment.volume`, the clip's own volume as stored, both last in its members and in its initialiser, where they are false and 1 unless given. `SoundPlan.clipVolume` is unchanged, so what is heard of a segment is `sound.clipVolume` times `segment.volume`. A photo is fitted into the canvas by its upright size like any clip.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Seeds/TestPhoto.swift`: `TestPhoto`, a test photo of one colour: its name, upright width and height, and colour. `info` is computed from them: the id is the name, the file name is the name with ".mov", its still movie. The picture itself is the name with ".png". The kind is `photo`, the duration `photoLongest`. `magenta` is 1080 by 1440 in 255, 0, 255. No file is written here.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Seeds/Seed.swift`: the seed `photo-wide` (`Seed.photoWide`) is blue, the magenta photo for 1800 ticks and red, each from its start, 6000 ticks on the landscape canvas, so its files are `blue.mov`, `magenta.mov` and `red.mov`. The photo stands at x 555, y 0, 810 by 1080. `Seed.testPhotos` is magenta for this seed and empty for the others.
- The three word files hold four groups for the words of clips, `add-clips`, `clip-panel`, `music-row` and `clip-path`, after the groups of the drafts. Each holds the keys of the part that fills it, and a group whose part is not built yet holds no key.

### Framing in the model and the plan

Every clip has a framing: how its picture stands on the canvas. The standard framing is the centred fit of before, to the bit, so a project that never changes a framing gives the plan it gave.

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Model/Framing.swift`: `Fit`, `fit` (whole inside the canvas) or `fill` (covering it), and `Backdrop`, `black` or `blurred` (the picture itself filling the canvas, under the bars). `Framing` holds a fit, a backdrop, a zoom from `leastZoom` 1 to `mostZoom` 4, and an offset `x` and `y`: the picture's centre from the canvas centre as a share of the canvas width and height, `y` positive down. `Framing.standard` is fit, black, zoom 1, offset 0. `held(uprightWidth:uprightHeight:canvas:)` holds the zoom from 1 to 4, then on each axis the offset, so the picture's near edge lies between `min(0, W minus w)` and `max(0, W minus w)`: a picture larger than the canvas never opens a gap, and one that fits whole never leaves it. An offset inside its limits is kept as it is.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Model/Project.swift`: `Clip.framing`, last in its members and in its initialiser, where it is the standard framing unless given, and read with `decodeIfPresent`, so a draft written before it reads as the standard framing. `sameFootage(as:)` compares the clips without their framing, so a change of framing alone, like a change of texts, refreshes the standing item and builds nothing.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Plan/RenderPlan.swift`: the rectangle of each segment is the fit size or the fill size of its upright picture, times the zoom, its centre at the picture centre plus the offset times the picture size, with the stored framing held again and its edges held again in pixels of the plan. Offsets are shares, so a plan at any export size scales with them. `Segment.backdrop`, last in its members and in its initialiser, where it is nil unless given, is the fill rectangle at zoom 1, centred, when the backdrop is blurred and the picture leaves part of the canvas bare, and nil otherwise.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/FrameAction.swift` and `FrameEdits.swift`: `EditorAction.frame(FrameAction)`, which `reduce` hands to `reduceFrame`. `setFit` and `setBackdrop` set one clip's fit and backdrop, `setZoom` its zoom held from 1 to 4, `setOffset` its offset, and `applyToAll` gives every clip the fit and the backdrop of the named clip and keeps each one's zoom and offset. Every framing an action changes is stored held. An unknown id and a value that is not finite change nothing, an action that changes the state pauses, and each is one step of undo, as every action is. A split gives both halves the framing of the clip, a move and the cuts keep it, and `addClips` gives the standard framing. No other action changes a framing.
- `EditorState.framedByHand` is the selected clip while no text is selected and the playhead is inside that clip, and nil otherwise. `EditorState.frameInfo` is "none" with no clip selected, else "<fit or fill> <black or blur> <zoom times 100> <x times 1000>,<y times 1000>" of the selected clip in whole numbers, as "fit black 100 0,0". Digits 0 to 9, the same in every language.
- The three word files end with three more groups after `clip-path`: `framing`, `guide` and `fold-frames`.

### The editor state and the edits

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/EditorState.swift`: `EditorState`, the project with selection, selected text, playhead and playing.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/EditorAction.swift`: `EditorAction`, everything a screen can ask for.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/Reduce.swift`: `reduce`, state plus action gives the new state. An action that cannot be done returns the state unchanged.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/Edits.swift`: the bodies of `addClips`, `split`, `trimStart`, `trimEnd` and `deleteSelected`, which `reduce` calls, and of `canSplit` and `canDelete`. An edit that cannot be done returns the state unchanged. A trim past a limit stops at the limit. An edit that is done pauses, a media id that a clip still uses keeps its stored facts when it is added again, and a delete keeps the facts of the deleted clip's media. Every edit keeps a valid state valid: each clip inside its source, each length whole frames and at least `TimelineGeometry.minimumClip`, the playhead a whole frame inside the project, the selection a clip of the project or none. An edit never changes the canvas.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/TimelineGeometry.swift`: `TimelineGeometry`, the scale of the timeline strip when an editor opens (60 points a second), the shortest clip (60 ticks), and the pure functions between a time and a point and from a drag in points to a trim amount at that scale. `TimelineScale` in the same file has the same functions at any scale from 10 to 240 points a second, as "The scale of the strip" says.

### Moving and cutting a clip

`Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/ClipEdits.swift` holds three clip edits, which `reduce` calls, and the values the clip panel and the strip read. Each edit that changes the project is one step of undo, as every action is.

- `moveClip(id, to: position)` takes the clip to a position from 1, held between 1 and the count of clips. It keeps the playhead and the texts at their times, carries the clip with its range and its volume, selects the clip and pauses. An unknown id and the position the clip has return the state unchanged, the playing flag included.
- `cutBefore` and `cutAfter` act on the selected clip at the playhead. `canCutBefore` and `canCutAfter` are true when the playhead is inside the selected clip with at least `minimumClip` on both sides, as `canSplit` asks. `cutBefore` takes away the part before the playhead and puts the playhead at the start of that clip. `cutAfter` takes away the part after it and leaves the playhead where it is. Both keep the selection and pause. While a cut cannot be done it returns the state unchanged. A photo is cut as any clip.
- `showsClipPanel` is true while the selection names a clip of the project. `showsClipVolume` is true while it is, and the media of that clip has sound, so never for a photo.
- `Project.dropPosition(of:at:)` is the position from 1 a clip takes when it is let go at a time: 1 plus the count of the other clips, in their order with the clip taken out, whose middle is before the time. Nil for an unknown id.

### Undo and redo

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/History.swift`: `Editing` holds the `EditorState`, an `UndoHistory` and the flag `unsaved`. `after(_:)` sends an action through `reduce`, which stays as it is, and `undone()` and `redone()` are functions of `Editing`, never actions. The history holds whole projects, never inverse actions: `past` and `future`, each project without its blank texts (`withoutBlankTexts`), at most `UndoHistory.depth` (50) steps, the oldest dropped.
- One step is one change of the project without its blank texts, compared with `nextTextID` left out. So a text added and left blank is no step, a text added and typed is one, and the project kept keeps its own `nextTextID`, so a new text never takes an id in use. The playhead, playing, the selection of a clip or a text and the sound panel are never part of a step.
- A run (`EditRun`) is a row of actions on one text of one kind, and is one step: typing (`setString`) or shaping (`move`, `turn`, and a `setStyle` that changes nothing but the size and the line spacing). Any other action ends it, `pause` included, which every gesture and every trim sends first. A new step empties `future`.
- An undo or a redo keeps the selection of a clip or a text that is still in the project and clears the other, holds the playhead inside the project on a whole frame, pauses, and closes the sound panel when the project has no clip. `writesNow` is true when something changed since `written()` and no run is open.

### Levels that follow the finger

A level slider sends each value of a drag, so the level she hears follows her finger while the video plays on, and the whole drag is one step of undo and one write of the draft.

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/History.swift`: `EditRun.drag`, a run opened only by `Editing.afterDragStep(_:)`, which is `after(_:)` with the run `drag`: the steps of one drag that change the project are one step. `endingRun()` gives the editing with no run and all else kept. While a drag run is open `writesNow` is false. An action that leaves the project unchanged never ends a drag run, so the reports of the playhead (`movePlayhead`) that the player sends every frame while playing never split a drag. Only `endingRun()`, an undo, a redo, or an action sent by `after(_:)` that changes the project ends it. `EditRun.of` is unchanged, so every other send is one step as before. A draft written with an open drag run opens with no run, as for the other runs.
- `App/Editor/EditorStore.swift`: `sendDragStep(_:)` sends a step by `afterDragStep` and writes nothing. `endDrag()` ends the run and writes the draft when `writesNow` holds.
- `App/Editor/Sound/SoundSlider.swift` takes, last, `follows` (false by default) and `ended`. With `follows` the slider calls `send` at every change of a drag and `ended` when the drag ends, and after a change with no drag calls `send`, then `ended`. A slider that goes from the screen during a drag, as the clip slider does when another clip is selected, calls `ended` then, so no run stays open. The five level sliders pass `follows: true`, sending by `sendDragStep` and ending by `endDrag`: `sound.clipVolume`, `sound.musicVolume`, `sound.fadeIn`, `sound.fadeOut` and `clip.volume`. `sound.musicStart` sends once, at the end of its drag, and builds a new item as before.
- Each value of a drag reaches the player as a level, so `follow` gives the standing item the new mix with `refreshSound`, which keeps the item's sound tap and changes only its gains, and builds nothing. Whether a slow drag is heard with no click or stepping is judged on the phone.

### The render plan

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Plan/RenderPlan.swift`: `renderPlan(for:)` turns a project into segments, each with its source range and the rectangle its upright picture fills on the canvas of the project. The canvas in the plan: `renderPlan(for:)` fits each upright picture whole inside `canvas.width` by `canvas.height` of the project, centred, and the side that limits the fit takes the canvas value itself, at the standard framing. Another framing places it as "Framing in the model and the plan" says. It copies the canvas into `RenderPlan.canvas`, for an empty project too. A plan built with no canvas is `portrait`. The numbers of a `Placement` are pixels of the plan's picture, `width` by `height`, which at `standard` is the canvas the plan carries.

### Export settings in the plan

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Export/ExportSettings.swift`: `ExportSize`, the size of an export by its short side, `hd720`, `hd1080` and `uhd4K` (720, 1080 and 2160 pixels), and `ExportRate`, `thirty` and `sixty` frames a second. Each case gives its word key (`export.size.720`, `export.size.1080`, `export.size.4K`, `export.rate.30`, `export.rate.60`), and `allWords` of each type lists its keys in the order of its cases. `ExportSize.rates` is the rates offered at a size: 30 and 60 at every size. `ExportSettings` is a size and a rate. `standard` is 1080p at 30, what every export was before the choice and what the preview always is, and `startChoice`, what the export button holds when the editor opens, is `standard`. `width(on:)` and `height(on:)` are the sides of a canvas times the short side, divided by 1080: 1280 by 720, 1920 by 1080 and 3840 by 2160 on `landscape`, 720 by 1280, 1080 by 1920 and 2160 by 3840 on `portrait`. `choosing(_:)` with a size keeps the rate when that size offers it and else takes 30, and with a rate takes it when the size offers it and else changes nothing.
- `RenderPlan.settings` holds the settings of a plan, `standard` for a plan built with none, and `width`, `height` and `framesPerSecond` are computed from them and the canvas. `renderPlan(for:settings:)` copies the settings into the plan, for an empty project too, and fits each upright picture inside `width` by `height` as it fitted it inside the canvas before. It turns the centre and the font size of each text from canvas pixels into pixels of the picture: times the short side, then divided by 1080, kept as the `Double` that gives. At a short side of 1080 they are copied, not scaled, so the plan of `standard` is exactly the plan of before. `lineWidth` is the plan's width times `wrapShare`. The times, the media, the turns, `hasSound`, the duration, the canvas, the sound and every other member of a text are those of the plan at `standard`. `renderPlan(for:)` is `renderPlan(for:settings:)` at `standard`, so the preview and every caller of it get 1080p at 30. The model and the edits stay in canvas pixels and in frames of 30.

### The start state

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Start/StartState.swift`: `StartState`, the one value the start screen draws, with the cases `idle`, `copying`, `failed` and `ready(Project)`. `words` gives the word key of a case: `start.copying` while copying, `start.noneReadable` when failed, none for the other two. `allWords` lists those two keys. `after(_:)` is the one function that moves between the cases, on a `StartState.Event`: `pickerClosed(chosen:)` with the count of chosen videos (none leaves `idle`, one or more give `copying`), `copyingEnded` with the facts that could be read (they are added to an empty project with `addClips`: a project with a clip gives `ready` with that project, an empty one gives `failed`), and `failureConfirmed` (from `failed` back to `idle`). An event that has no meaning in a case leaves it unchanged. A video that could not be copied or read is simply not among the facts.

### The export state

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Export/ExportState.swift`: `ExportState`, the one value the export button and its panel draw, with the cases `idle`, `exporting(percent:)`, `saved` and `failed(notAllowed:)`. `words` is nil for `idle`, then `Words("export.exporting")`, `Words("export.saved")`, and `Words("export.failed")` or `Words("export.notAllowed")`. `percentText` is the percent while exporting, such as "42%", written with the digits 0 to 9, and nil in every other case. `allWords` lists those four keys. `canStart(_:)` takes the `EditorState` and is true exactly when `canExport` is true and the case is `idle`. `after(_:)` moves between the cases on an `ExportState.Event` (`started`, `progress`, `saved`, `failed(notAllowed:)`, `closed`). A progress value is held between 0 and 1 and never lowers the percent, and an event with no meaning in a case leaves it unchanged, so a progress value that arrives after the end changes nothing.

### The launch values

### Drafts

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Drafts/Draft.swift`: `Draft`, the stored form of a project: `version`, the project, its `exportSettings` and `changed`, the time of the write. A new draft has `Draft.version`, 1. The model types, `RGB` and the three types of the export settings are `Codable` as Swift writes it, in the file of each type, so a draft has no coding of its own. `encodeDraft` writes it as JSON with sorted keys and the standard coding of a date. `decodeDraft` reads it back and throws `DraftError.unreadable` for bytes that are not the JSON of a draft and for a version above `Draft.version`. `DraftID` is the name of the folder of a draft, a UUID string. A draft also holds the undo history in `history`, with `UndoHistory` and `EditRun` `Codable`: nil reads as an empty history, and a file without it reads as nil. `Editing(state:history:)` opens an editing on a history read back: the state as given, the history with its run ended, and `unsaved` false.
- The media facts of a project are a dictionary whose key is no string, so they are stored as one array of key and value in the order of the dictionary, which can differ from one process to the next. Drafts are compared as values, never as bytes.
- `Project.fileNames` is the file name of every media a clip uses and of the music, each once, sorted. A media fact that no clip uses names no file. `keepingFiles(_:)` takes out every clip whose media has no facts or whose file name is not among the names given, every media fact that no kept clip uses, and the music when its file name is not there. It keeps the canvas, the texts, the ids, the volume and the mute.
- `DraftSummary` is what the list shows of one draft: its id, `changed`, the length, the count of clips and of texts, the export choice and whether it has a cover. `DraftSummary.unreadable` has no date, a length of zero, no clip, no text and `standard`, and its `isReadable` is false. `dateText(_:in:)` writes a date in a zone as "2026-10-14 21:05": the Gregorian calendar, 24 hours, the digits 0 to 9 in every language. `draftsInfo(_:)` is the line a UI test reads: the count, then one field for each summary in its order, one space between them. A readable one is `<clips>+<length in ticks>+<texts>+<short side>x<frames a second>+<c or .>`, c when it has a cover, an unreadable one is `x`, and none gives `0`.
- `LaunchOptions.drafts` is `fresh` or `kept` for `-drafts fresh` or `-drafts kept`, and nil for none and for another value. `showsInfo` is true with a seed or with drafts. `draftsFolder(applicationSupport:caches:)` is `<caches>/TestDrafts` with drafts set and `<applicationSupport>/Drafts` without.
- The three word files end with five groups after `export-path`: `undo`, `draft-saving`, `project-list`, `draft-delete` and `draft-path`, the last one empty.

### The draft shelf

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Drafts/DraftShelf.swift`: `DraftShelf` and `OpenDraft`, the only code that makes, reads, writes or removes anything in the folder of drafts. They use `FileManager` and `Data` alone. A draft is one folder `<drafts>/<id>/`, where `<drafts>` is `<Application Support>/Drafts/` on the phone and `<caches>/TestDrafts/` on a launch with `-drafts`, and `<id>` is a UUID string. It holds `draft.json`, the folder `Media/` with a copy of every video and music file the project names, and, once written, `cover.jpg`. `mediaFolder(of:)` and `coverFile(of:)` give the last two.
- `create` makes `<drafts>/<id>/Media` with every folder above it under a new id and writes `draft.json`. When either fails, the new folder is removed again, so no draft is left. `OpenDraft.write` writes `encodeDraft` of a draft to `draft.json` with the atomic option of `Data.write`: a temporary name in the same folder, then a move over the old file, on Apple and on Linux alike.
- `list` gives one `DraftSummary` for each folder in `<drafts>`, newest `changed` first and a tie by id, then the drafts whose `draft.json` is missing or does not decode, as unreadable, by id. `hasCover` is whether `cover.jpg` is there. A folder of drafts that is not there gives none, and one that cannot be read gives none and a log line.
- `open` throws `unreadable` for a draft that cannot be read, and then touches no file. It also throws `unreadable`, touching no file, when `Media/` is there but cannot be listed, while a `Media/` that is not there reads as empty. Otherwise it repairs the draft before the editor sees it: `keepingFiles` of the names in `Media/` takes out every clip and the music whose file is not there. Then it removes from `Media/` every file that no project names, and logs with `NSLog` each clip, music and file it takes out. The projects that name a file are the project and every project of the stored undo history (`past` and `future`), and the history is dropped (`history` nil, with a log line) when the project lost a clip or its music, or when a project of the history names a file that is not there.
- Once a draft is made, a file of it is removed only by `open`, as above, and by `delete`, which removes `<drafts>/<id>` whole. `removeAll` removes `<drafts>` whole, for `-drafts fresh`. Every throw of the shelf is a `DraftError`: `unreadable` from `open`, `cannotWrite` from `create`, `write`, `delete` (also for an id that is not there) and `removeAll`.

## The Engine

`Sources/ReelsmithEngine` is the second package target and the only one that touches AVFoundation. It is the library product `ReelsmithEngine`, which the app links, and it depends on `ReelsmithCore`. It uses frameworks that exist on iOS and macOS alike, never UIKit or SwiftUI. Every file that imports an Apple framework sits whole inside `#if canImport(AVFoundation)`, and `Compositor.swift`, `PhotoImport.swift`, `TextPicture.swift` and `Fonts.swift` inside `#if canImport(CoreText)`, so Linux compiles nothing of them. The Engine and its tests are in Swift 5 language mode.

The Engine turns a render plan into video. Both entry points for that, `makePlayerItem` and `export`, take the plan, the facts of its media and the media folder, and find the file of a media id at `<media folder>/<fileName>`.

- `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/EngineError.swift`: `EngineError`, what an entry point can throw. Its case `notBuilt` is thrown by nothing.
- `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/MediaProbe.swift`: `readMediaInfo` reads the facts of the file `<media folder>/<fileName>` and returns them under the id and the file name it was given: the width and height of the picture as stored, the length of the picture track rounded down to a tick, the quarter turns, and whether the file has a sound track. The internal `quarterTurns(of:)` in the same file maps the stored transform of the picture track to the turns: b 1 is one turn, a minus 1 two turns, b minus 1 three turns, anything else none. The internal `wholeTicks(of:)` beside it rounds the length of the track down to a tick. It throws `sourceMissing` with the id when the file is not in the folder, and `cannotRead` when the file cannot be opened as a video or has no picture track.
- `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/Composition.swift`: `makePlayerItem` gives the player item of a plan, on the main actor, and `refreshPicture` gives a standing item a new plan ("Text in the frame" below). The internal `buildComposition` in the same file is the one builder for the player item and for the export. It makes a composition with one picture track and, when a segment has sound, one sound track that ends where the last segment with sound ends, one more sound track when the plan has music, and the audio mix of those sound tracks ("Sound in the composition" below). Its video composition, which the private `videoComposition(plan:media:tracks:kept:)` makes for a new item, an export and a refresh alike, has the size of the plan, `width` by `height`, at its `framesPerSecond`, with one `PieceInstruction` per segment, whose transform applies the quarter turns of the segment's placement to the stored picture, scales it to the placement's size and moves it to the placement's corner, with a flip before and after it, because the plan counts from the top left and Core Image from the bottom left. The video composition names `Compositor` through `customVideoCompositorClass`, so the compositor draws every frame, for the player item and the export alike. The canvas stays black around the picture, but under a blurred backdrop ("Framing in the picture" below). Nothing else in the Engine names a size, so `makePlayerItem` and `export` take no size, and the exported file has the size and the rate of its plan ("Export at a size and a rate" below). The video composition carries the 709 colour properties, so a high dynamic range source is converted to standard range for the preview and the export alike (a test holds it for the export). It throws `sourceMissing` with the media id when the plan names media with no facts or a file that is not in the media folder, and `cannotRead` when a file has no picture track or cannot be put into the composition.
- `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/Compositor.swift` holds the two types that draw the picture. `PieceInstruction` holds the time range of its segment, the id of the picture track, the transform that puts the source frame at its place, `backdrop`, the transform of its blurred copy or nil, and the texts of the plan whose time overlaps its range, each with its picture ("Text in the frame" below). It answers true for `containsTweening`, so every frame of its range is asked for with its own time. `Compositor` conforms to `AVVideoCompositing`. It asks for source frames and for its own buffers as 32 bit BGRA with the IOSurface key (`kCVPixelBufferPixelFormatTypeKey` is `kCVPixelFormatType_32BGRA`, `kCVPixelBufferIOSurfacePropertiesKey` is an empty dictionary). The order of a frame: black, then the blurred backdrop when the instruction has one, then the source frame of the segment through the transform of its instruction, then the texts of the instruction whose time holds the frame, cut to the canvas. It draws with Core Image with colour management off: the context has no working and no output colour space and keeps no intermediate image between frames, and a source frame and a text picture are read with none, so a colour leaves with the numbers it came with. `supportsHDRSourceFrames` and `supportsWideColorSourceFrames` are not implemented, and the video composition keeps the 709 properties. A time at which the picture track has no frame (a track whose first frame comes after the start of its file) is no error: the frame is the black canvas under its texts. A frame request that cannot be answered (no instruction of ours, no buffer from the render context) is finished with `EngineError.cannotRead`, and an export then ends in `cannotWrite`. Every request is answered before `startRequest` returns.
- `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/Export.swift`: `export` writes the plan as an MPEG-4 file with AAC through an export session, which gets the audio mix of the builder: with H.264 through the highest quality preset, and with HEVC through the HEVC highest quality preset when the short side of the plan's size is 2160. The sound ends where the last clip with sound ends or where the music ends, whichever is later. It writes under a temporary name in the folder of the output place and renames when the file is whole, which replaces a file that was there. `progress` gets 0 before the export starts, the session's own value about ten times a second while it only rises, and 1 after the rename. It may be called from any thread. An empty plan throws `nothingToExport`. A folder of the output place that is missing or cannot be written throws `cannotWrite` before any work, and so does an export session that fails or a rename that fails. A session that fails because it cannot decode (`AVError.decodeFailed`) is logged, its temporary file removed, and run once more from the same composition and settings: a second failure throws `cannotWrite`, and any other failure throws at once. A failed export leaves no file of its own behind.
- `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/PlayerTime.swift` holds one extension of `AVPlayer` with three functions on the main actor, each in `Time` (600 ticks a second). `seekExactly(to:)` seeks with tolerance zero before and after and reports nothing, and may be sent before the item is ready. `seekExactly(to:then:)` does the same seek and calls `done` on the main actor: true when the seek landed, false when another seek ended it. `reportTime(every:_:)` reports the player's time on the main queue, rounded toward zero to a tick, and its result goes to `removeTimeObserver`. The three convert between `Time` and the player's time, at the scale 600. A new item does not end a seek. A seek followed at once by `replaceCurrentItem(with:)` still lands on the old item and `done` gets true while the player already holds the new item. So `done(true)` does not mean the player's present item stands on that time, and the caller keeps its own guard. The preview calls `seekExactly(to:then:)` and `reportTime(every:_:)`, and drops the result of a seek of an older build by its build number. The file also holds one extension of `AVPlayerItem` with one function on the main actor, `seekExactly(to:)`: the item's own seek with tolerance zero before and after and no completion handler, for an item that is in no player yet. Sent before the item goes into a player it gives the item its starting time, so the player makes the item ready on that time with no seek of its own. The platform's page of that seek says nothing of an item that is in no player or not ready: the package test of the second item holds it on the hosted Mac.
- `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/TestClipMaker.swift`: `writeTestClip` writes one test clip from its Core values as `<folder>/<name>.mov`: H.264 with the 709 colour properties, and for a clip with sound one mono track of uncompressed 16 bit samples. It makes the folder when missing, writes under a temporary name in that folder, renames, and keeps a file that is already there. A clip with one quarter turn gets the stored transform a 0, b 1, c minus 1, d 0, tx the stored height, ty 0. Other turns are written with no transform.

### Faces and the picture of a text

The folder `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/Fonts/` holds three files and nothing else, each as it lies in the archive of the release 1.003 of https://github.com/aliftype/amiri, unchanged: `Amiri-Regular.ttf` (437,780 bytes), `Amiri-Bold.ttf` (414,560 bytes) and `OFL.txt` (4,389 bytes), the SIL Open Font License 1.1 of the family. The two font files of the release archive are smaller than those of the folder `fonts` of that repository, and the archive is what is committed. `Package.swift` gives the Engine target the folder as a resource, `resources: [.copy("Fonts")]`, so the files lie in the bundle of the target under `Fonts/`. They are the only binary files in git, and no font is fetched at build time.

- `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/Fonts.swift`: the internal `font(_:size:)` gives the face of a `TextFont` at a size in pixels of the plan's picture. On its first call it registers the two font files for the process, from the bundle of the Engine target. `naskh` is the face with the PostScript name "Amiri-Regular" and `naskhBold` the one with "Amiri-Bold", the names the two files give. `system` is the face the platform gives for its own screens, and no system face is named by its name. A name that is not registered is answered by the platform with another face, so the name of the face that came back is compared, and a bundled face that cannot be made gives the system face. That fallback covers a face that cannot be made from its file: a resource bundle that is not inside the app stops the app at the first call, in the accessor the package makes for the bundle. The public `faceName(of:)` is the PostScript name of the face that was really made, after that fallback.
- `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/TextPicture.swift`: the one place where text is laid out and drawn, by Core Text. The internal `textBlock(for:)` lays out the string of a `TextLayer`. Every line as typed is a paragraph of its own, so its direction is left to its own text. A line longer than `lineWidth` wraps there, and no canvas is read. The width of the block is that of its widest line without the space a line ends with. `align` puts every line against the left of the block, its middle or its right, in every language. A line is as high as its ascent, descent and leading, and `lineSpacing` times the size is added between two lines. A line with nothing typed keeps the height of a line.
- The internal `textPicture(for:)` in the same file returns nil for a string that is empty or holds only spaces and line breaks, and never throws. The rectangle of a text picture is the block grown by 0.3 of the size on every side: `width` and `height` are its size in pixels of the plan's picture, and the image has that many pixels, each count rounded up, with the block in its middle. So the centre of the image is the centre of the block. The image is 8 bit red, green, blue and alpha in sRGB, with the colour of the text drawn by the numbers of its `RGB`, and is clear outside the letters, their outline, their shadow and the box. With `box` on the whole image is black at 60 percent, with square corners. With `outline` on a line lies behind the letters and shows 0.08 of the size around them, black, and white when the colour of the text is black. With `shadow` on the letters and their outline cast one shadow, black at 60 percent, moved down by 0.06 of the size and blurred by 0.12 of the size. A picture depends on the string, the style and the wrap width alone.

### Tones and music files

Music is a file she picked herself, copied into the media folder of the project. A tone for a test is made in code, and no sound file is in git. Both files below sit whole inside `#if canImport(AVFoundation)`.

- `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/TestToneMaker.swift`: `writeTestTone(_:to:)` writes a `TestTone` of Core as `<folder>/<name>.mov`, the way the clip maker writes the sound of a clip: a QuickTime file with one sound track and no picture, uncompressed 16 bit samples at the rate and with the channels of the tone, a sine at its first pitch for the first half of its length and at its second pitch for the rest, with its peak, exactly as long as its ticks. It makes the folder when missing, writes under a temporary name in that folder, renames, and keeps a file that is already there.
- `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/MusicProbe.swift`: `readMusicFile(id:fileName:title:in:)` reads the file `<media folder>/<fileName>` and returns a `MusicFile` under the id, the file name and the title given, with the length of its first sound track rounded down to a tick. It asks the asset for `hasProtectedContent`, `isComposable` and `isExportable` with `load(_:)`, and the internal `musicUse(hasSound:isProtected:isComposable:isExportable:)` beside it decides from those flags and whether there is a sound track.
- `importMusic(from:title:to:)` in the same file copies a picked file to `<media folder>/<a new UUID string>.<its extension>`, or with no extension when the source has none, then reads it with `readMusicFile` under that UUID string as its id. It makes the media folder when missing. It holds the right to read the source only while it copies: it starts the security scoped access, tries the copy whatever that start answered (a file that needs no right answers false), and ends the access when the start said yes. The title is the one given, kept exactly, or the name of the source without its extension. The source stays where it was.
- The refusals, as `EngineError`: `cannotRead` for a file that cannot be opened, one with no sound track, one whose first sound track has no length, and a copy that fails. `protectedFile`, the case this adds to the error, for a file that is protected, or one with sound that cannot go into a composition or an export. `sourceMissing` with the id from `readMusicFile` for a file name that is not in the folder. After any throw of `importMusic` no copy is left in the media folder. A video with sound is accepted: its sound is the music.

### Facts of a video file

`Sources/ReelsmithEngine/VideoFacts.swift`, whole inside `#if canImport(AVFoundation)`, reads what a written video holds. `readVideoFacts(of:)` loads the first picture track of the file and returns a `VideoFacts`: `width` and `height` of the picture as stored (`naturalSize`), and `framesPerSecond`, the nominal rate of the track rounded to a whole number. `infoText` writes the three numbers with a space between them, as "1280 720 60", in the digits 0 to 9. It throws `cannotRead` for a file that cannot be opened or has no picture track, as a tone file.

### Text in the frame

The compositor draws the texts of the render plan into every frame, for the player item and the export alike. Nothing but the plan tells it what to draw.

- Where a picture is made: the internal `pieceTexts(of:in:kept:)` in `Compositor.swift` is the one place that calls `textPicture(for:)`. `videoComposition(plan:media:tracks:kept:)` in `Composition.swift` calls it once for each segment when a video composition is built. Each `PieceInstruction` keeps the result in `texts`: one `PieceText` for every layer of the plan whose time overlaps the range of the instruction, in the order of the plan. So the two instructions of `text-wide` keep one each, and of `text-box-wide` the first keeps one and the second none. A layer with no picture is left out: a blank string in a plan built by hand, or a bitmap that cannot be made.
- Under what it is kept: a `PieceText` holds, beside its picture, the string, the style and the wrap width the picture was drawn from, and nothing else of the layer. A layer with the same three takes the picture that is there, from the video composition before it or from an earlier instruction of the same build, and nothing is drawn. So the text of `text-wide` is drawn once for its two instructions, and a text that only moved or turned is not drawn again. A picture no layer of the new plan asks for is not carried on. `drawn` of an instruction counts the calls of `textPicture(for:)` its build made.
- The place: the image is put by its centre at `x`, `y` of the layer, with the pixel size of the image, y counted from the top of the plan's picture, and turned around that centre, a positive rotation clockwise as she sees it. Core Image counts from the bottom left, so the centre goes to the plan's `height` less `y` and the angle is negated. At a rotation of 0 the top left corner of the image is rounded to a whole pixel, so the letters stay sharp. The picture enters Core Image with no colour space, as a source frame does, so a text colour is written with the numbers of its `RGB`. A `PieceText` holds the picture as placed, made when the video composition is built.
- The order of a frame: black, then the blurred backdrop when the instruction has one, then the source frame of the segment, then each text of the instruction whose time holds the frame, in the order of the plan, the later one on top. A layer is drawn in a frame at time t when `timelineStart <= t < timelineStart + duration`. `startRequest` compares the time of the frame with the two ends a `PieceText` holds and puts the placed picture on. It lays out, measures and draws nothing for text and never calls `textPicture(for:)`.
- `refreshPicture(of:plan:media:)` in `Composition.swift`, public and on the main actor, gives a standing player item a new video composition built from a plan, so the texts of the plan are drawn from the next frame on, with no new item. It loads no file and reads no asset: the picture track of each segment and the pictures to use again come from the instructions the item has. It takes an item that `makePlayerItem` made and a plan with as many segments as that item has instructions. For another item, another number of segments, or a segment whose time range differs from the range of its instruction, it throws `cannotRead` before anything is set, the item keeps the video composition it had, and the caller builds a new item. A plan that names media with no facts throws `sourceMissing`, as `makePlayerItem` does. The preview calls it when only the texts of the project changed ("The preview and text" under the app).

### Photos in the picture

A photo comes into the media folder as a still movie, so the composition, the compositor, the cover and the export take a photo clip as they take a clip of a video: its picture track is inserted from the movie and the compositor draws its frame at its place. No screen draws it.

- `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/PhotoImport.swift`: `importPhoto(from:id:into:)` reads a picture with its orientation applied, scales it so its long side is at most 3840 pixels and both sides are even, and writes it upright as the movie `<folder>/<id raw>.mov`: one picture track at a time scale of 600 with the 709 colour properties, H.264 while the picture has at most 36864 blocks of 16 by 16 (3840 by 2160 has 32400) and HEVC above or when the H.264 write fails, since an encoder may refuse a tall picture, `MediaInfo.photoLongest` long, which holds the picture twice, at zero and one frame of 30 before the end, so it is small on disk. The metadata of the source is not carried, so nothing turns it again. It gives the `MediaInfo` of the movie: kind photo, the upright size, no turn, no sound, `photoLongest`. It throws `cannotRead` for a file that is no picture and `cannotWrite` when the movie cannot be written, and leaves nothing written then: the movie is written under a temporary name and renamed.
- `writeTestPhoto(_:to:)` writes a `TestPhoto` as `<folder>/<name>.png` in its one colour, through a temporary name, keeps a file that is there and makes a missing folder. The picture is what the photo library of the simulator gets. The seed start and the Engine tests make its movie with `importPhoto`.
- A plan that ends with a photo, begins with one or holds nothing else gives an item, an export and a cover of its whole length, as a plan of videos does.

### Framing in the picture

The compositor draws the framing the plan gives (rule 22): fill, zoom and offset reach the Engine as the rectangle of `Segment.placement`, and the blurred bars as `Segment.backdrop`. No screen draws either, and the preview, the cover and the export take them alike.

- Fill, zoom and offset need no drawing of their own: a placement larger than the canvas is put by the same transform and cut at the canvas as every frame is.
- `videoComposition(plan:media:tracks:kept:)` in `Composition.swift` counts the transform of `Segment.backdrop` as it counts that of the placement, and gives it to the instruction as `backdrop`, nil when the segment has none.
- `startRequest` of `Compositor` draws the backdrop first when the instruction has one: the segment's own source frame clamped at its edges, scaled to an eighth, blurred by `CIGaussianBlur` with a radius of 8 at that size, scaled back, put at the backdrop and cut to the canvas, so the edge where the fill meets the canvas is not darkened. The picture goes over it as before. A segment with no backdrop is drawn as before, with no blur.
- A change of framing alone keeps the footage the same, so the standing item gets `refreshPicture` with the new plan and its instructions hold the new transforms. Nothing is built.

### Sound in the composition

Every sample of the preview and of the export comes from the composition of the one builder and its audio mix, made from `RenderPlan.sound`. Nothing but the plan says how loud anything is, and the platform mixes. The one code of our own that touches a sample is the tap of the clip track, as "A clip above full volume" says.

- The sound of the clips stays one composition track. When `plan.sound.music` is set, `buildComposition` in `Composition.swift` lays the range of the file `<media folder>/<fileName>` from `sourceStart` for `duration` at zero on a second sound track. The music is never longer than the project, so the picture keeps its length. A music file that is not in the media folder throws `sourceMissing` with the id of the layer, from `makePlayerItem` and from `export` alike, and one with no sound track throws `cannotRead`.
- The builder returns `audioMix` as a third member beside the composition and the video composition: one `AVMutableAudioMix` with one `AVMutableAudioMixInputParameters`, made with the plain `init()` and its `trackID` set, for each sound track there is. The clips are held as "The volume of each clip in the mix" says, which is 0 when they are muted. A plan with `SoundPlan.plain` and every clip at 1 holds them at 1 and has no music, so it sounds as it did before.
- The music rises from 0 to its `volume` over the first `fadeIn` and falls from it to 0 over the last `fadeOut`, each by `setVolumeRamp(fromStartVolume:toEndVolume:timeRange:)`, and stands at `volume` between them. With a fade in of zero it stands at `volume` from zero on, and with a fade out of zero no ramp is set at its end. Both fades are cut to half of the music in the plan, so the two ramps never overlap.
- `makePlayerItem` gives the item the mix as its `audioMix`, and `export` gives it to its session. A change of the music's file or start builds a new item (`sameFootage` is false), a change of a level gives the standing item a new mix as "Levels while playing" says, and `refreshPicture` leaves the mix alone.
- The exported file has one AAC sound track, which the session mixes from the two. It takes the higher rate and the larger channel count of its sources: stereo music at 48000 samples a second over clips at 44100 in mono gives a file at 48000 in stereo.

### The volume of each clip in the mix

- The whole mix of an item and of an export is made by one function, `audioMix(for:clipTrack:musicTrack:)` in `Composition.swift`, private to the file. `buildComposition` calls it with the ids of the clip sound track and of the music track, either nil when there is none. It reads no file: it makes its parameters from the plan alone. `addMusic` only lays the music on its track and gives its id.
- The clip track gets one `setVolumeRamp(fromStartVolume:toEndVolume:timeRange:)` for each segment that has sound, in the order of the plan, from `plan.sound.clipVolume * segment.volume`, held at 1 at most, to the same value over the time range of the segment on the timeline. What lies above 1 is the tap's ("A clip above full volume"). Two volumes set with `setVolume(_:at:)` are not held: the platform ramps from one to the next, and blue at 1 before red at 0.5 read 0.375 in its window. So what is heard of a clip is the volume of the clips times its own volume, and 0 while the clips are muted. A photo is a movie with no sound track and sets nothing. A segment with no sound lays nothing on the track, so the volume it would hold is never heard.
- The music's parameters are the ones under "Sound in the composition", made in the same function.

### Levels while playing

A level changed while the video plays leaves it playing. A level is the volume of the clips, the mute, the volume of a clip, the volume of the music and its two fades.

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/SoundEdits.swift`: `setClipVolume`, `muteClips`, `setVolumeOfClip`, `setMusicVolume`, `setFadeIn` and `setFadeOut` that change the state leave `isPlaying` as it was. `setMusic`, `removeMusic` and `setMusicStart` that change the state pause, as before.
- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Model/Project.swift`: `sameTracks(as:)` is `sameFootage(as:)` with the levels left out. It compares the canvas, the media, the clips without their volume, and the music's file and start, so it holds when only levels (and texts) differ.
- `refreshSound(of:plan:)` in `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/Composition.swift`, public and on the main actor, gives an item `makePlayerItem` made the mix of the plan, made by `audioMix(for:clipTrack:musicTrack:)`, the function of a build. The sound tracks of the item's composition, in the order of their ids, are the clip track when a segment has sound, then the music track when the plan has music. When the item is not a composition or its count of sound tracks does not match the plan it throws `cannotRead`, and the item keeps its mix. It reads no file.
- `App/Editor/Preview/PlayerPicture.swift`: when `sameTracks` holds and `sameFootage` does not, no build runs and an item stands, `follow` calls `refreshSound` on that item. The player is not stopped and the count of builds does not change. When the texts or the framings of the clips differ too, `refreshPicture` follows as for a change of the texts. When `refreshSound` throws, or a build runs, a new item is built.
- The level sliders send each value of a drag, as "Levels that follow the finger" says. Whether the sound follows the new mix at once, with no click or gap, is judged on the phone.

### A clip above full volume

The volume of a clip goes from 0 to 2, so a quiet clip can be heard at twice its level. No input of the mix is ever given a volume above 1: the gain above 1 is a tap on the clip track.

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/SoundEdits.swift`: `setVolumeOfClip` holds the volume between 0 and 2. `clipInfo` reads a clip at 2 as "200". `App/Editor/Clip/ClipPanel.swift`: the slider `clip.volume` goes from 0 to 2.
- `audioMix(for:clipTrack:musicTrack:)` in `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/Composition.swift`: for each segment that has sound, the clip track holds `clipVolume` times the segment's volume up to 1, and an `MTAudioProcessingTap` set as the `audioTapProcessor` of the clip track's parameters multiplies each sample by what lies above, `max(clipVolume * volume, 1)` for its segment, and presses it toward full scale with `ClipGains.limited`, as the next lines say. The tap reads the time of a sample from the time range of the source audio it is given, and holds the gains of the plan in `ClipGains`, private to the file, which it lets go when the platform ends it. Every mix carries a tap, so a gain of 1 only holds the samples at full scale, as the platform does.
- A build makes a new tap with the mix. `refreshSound` keeps the tap the item's clip track has and only replaces the gains it reads, under a lock (`OSAllocatedUnfairLock`), so the audio thread reads a whole array. A new tap would make the player prepare the item again, which stopped picture and sound for most of a second at each level in the simulator (reason `AudioProcessingTapChanged`). So the item and the export get the same gain, and a level above 1 is a refresh of the mix like any other. The count of sound tracks does not depend on any volume.
- A clip raised above 100 percent is pressed down smoothly near full scale, never cut flat. `ClipGains.limited` acts on each sample and its gain alone. At a gain of 1 it holds the sample between minus 1 and 1, as before, so a clip at or under 100 percent passes the tap as it did. Above 1 the sample times its gain goes through one curve: straight up to a knee, then the knee plus the room up to a ceiling times the tanh of what lies past the knee over that room. The curve meets the line with the same slope and never reaches its ceiling, so it is smooth in the sample, and every raised sample, a loud clip's own peaks too, ends under the ceiling. From a gain of 1.2 on the knee is 0.7 and the ceiling 0.9: a raised sample of 0.8 comes out at 0.79, of 1 at 0.88, of 1.5 and more at 0.90. The room below full scale is for the AAC encoder of an export, whose peaks rose 3 percent over those of the mix. Between a gain of 1 and 1.2 the knee moves from 1 down to 0.7 and the ceiling from 1 down to 0.9 in step with the gain, so 101 percent is the old clamp all but exactly. In that span a sample above about 0.72 falls a little as the gain rises, from its plain value to the curve, since it has to end under the ceiling. Above 1.2 every sample rises with the gain. It keeps no state, so the kept tap of a refresh needs nothing rebuilt, and it has no attack or release, so it cannot pump. The music has no tap and is not limited, nor is the sum of the clips and the music.
- The tap reads its audio as float samples and the time range it is given as the time of the composition. The levels of red in `ClipGainTests` hold only when both are so.

### Export at a size and a rate

The Engine draws the plan's size at the plan's rate, and reads both from the plan alone.

- `videoComposition(plan:media:tracks:kept:)` in `Composition.swift` sets `renderSize` to `plan.width` by `plan.height` and `frameDuration` to one over `plan.framesPerSecond`, and the flip after the placing transform names `plan.height`. `pieceTexts(of:in:kept:)` in `Compositor.swift` flips the place of a text with `plan.height`. The compositor draws every frame in the size of its render context, which is that render size.
- The Engine scales no number of a plan. The places of the pictures, the centre, the size and the wrap width of a text come in pixels of the plan's picture from `renderPlan(for:settings:)`, so a text at 4K is drawn by its picture at twice the size and is never enlarged. The model keeps its frame of 30: a rate of 60 changes only how many frames the file holds.
- `export` takes `AVAssetExportPresetHEVCHighestQuality` when `plan.settings.size.shortSide` is 2160 and `AVAssetExportPresetHighestQuality` otherwise, with the file type MPEG-4 at every size. On the hosted Mac the highest quality preset writes 720p and 1080p at 30 and 60 with H.264, but 4K at 30 only, and the HEVC preset writes 4K at 60 with every frame. An export the session cannot write throws `cannotWrite`, as before.
- The preview is not the export: `makePlayerItem` and `refreshPicture` get plans of `renderPlan(for:)`, which are 1920 by 1080 or 1080 by 1920 at 30, so the player item is as it was.

### The cover of a draft

The cover is the first frame of the plan as the compositor draws it, texts included, made small and written as a JPEG.

- `writeCover(plan:media:mediaFolder:to:)` in `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/Cover.swift`, public and off the main actor, builds through `buildComposition` and reads the frame at zero with an `AVAssetImageGenerator` given the video composition of that build, with both tolerances zero. There is no second builder, so the cover is the frame of the preview and of the export.
- Core Image scales the frame so that its long side is at most 360 pixels, with a high quality downsample, clamped at its edges so they are not blended with the clear outside, and crops it to whole pixels, which makes 360 by 202 on the landscape canvas. It writes it as a JPEG in sRGB under a temporary name beside the file, then moves it into place, or replaces a file that is there with `replaceItemAt`, which keeps the old cover when the replace fails. The whole file sits inside `#if canImport(CoreText)` and imports no ImageIO, UIKit or AppKit.
- It throws `nothingToExport` for a plan with no segment, `cannotWrite` when the folder of the file is not there or cannot be written, `sourceMissing` and `cannotRead` as `buildComposition` does, `cannotRead` when the generator gives no frame, and `cannotWrite` when the JPEG cannot be written or renamed. When it throws, nothing is written at the place asked.

## The app

Screens live in `App/`, hold no logic and no word, and draw a Core state. `project.yml` sets Swift 5 language mode for the app and the UI tests. `UITests/` is the UI test target `ReelsmithUITests` of `project.yml`. The scheme `Reelsmith` has a test action that runs it.

A slot is a place in a Swift file between the two comment lines `// slot: <name>` and `// end slot: <name>`, which mark where one part of the app is put into another.

### The root and the seed start

- `App/ReelsmithApp.swift`: the debug guard and the root. A debug build hands the process arguments to `LaunchOptions`, a release build an empty list. The root holds an optional `EditorStore`: with a store whose `isOpen` is true it shows `EditorScreen`, without one or with a closed one the content of the slot `start`, which is `StartScreen`. The editor is opened by setting that store. The initialiser also makes the `DraftShelf` on `options.draftsFolder(applicationSupport:caches:)` and, on `-drafts fresh`, empties it with `try? removeAll()` before any view reads it. The shelf goes to `seedStart` and to `StartScreen`.
- `App/SeedStart.swift`: `seedStart`, the start on a seed. It writes the seed's test clips with `writeTestClip`, its test tones (`Seed.testTones`) with `writeTestTone` and its test photos (`Seed.testPhotos`) with `writeTestPhoto`, then as still movies with `importPhoto`, into `<caches>/Seeds/`, where a file that is already there is kept. Without `-drafts` it returns a store on the seed's project with that folder as the media folder and no draft, so nothing is written. With `-drafts` it makes a new draft of the seed's project at `ExportSettings.startChoice` with `shelf.create`, copies each name of `project.fileNames` from `<caches>/Seeds/` into the draft's `Media/`, and returns a store on that media folder with the `OpenDraft`. A draft that cannot be made is logged, and the store opens on `<caches>/Seeds/` with no draft. The state starts with `selectedText` set to `Seed.selectedText`, so a start on a text seed has its text selected, and with `showsSound` set to `Seed.showsSound`, so a start on `music-wide` has the sound panel open. A playhead from `-playhead` goes through `reduce` with `movePlayhead`, so it is clamped to the project and rounded down to a frame. A clip, a tone or a photo that cannot be written does not stop the start: its file is missing from the folder and the error is logged with `NSLog`.

### The start screen

The start screen, in the folder `App/Start/`. It is the content of the slot `start` of `App/ReelsmithApp.swift`, shown while the root holds no store or a closed one.

Only when that gives `copying` it makes a new draft with `shelf.create`, an empty project of the chosen shape at `ExportSettings.startChoice`, and starts the copy into the draft's `Media/`. ### The editor store and the shell

- `App/Editor/EditorStore.swift`: `EditorStore`, the one observable object of the editor. It holds the `Editing` of Core, the media folder and the launch options. `state` is read from the editing, so every screen reads `store.state` as before. `send` is `editing = editing.after(action)`, and `undo` and `redo` are `editing.undone()` and `editing.redone()`, as "Undo beside play" says, each followed by the write of "Saving a draft".
- `App/Editor/EditorScreen.swift`: `EditorScreen`, the shell. Its root has the identifier `editor.root` and holds four regions from top to bottom, `editor.previewRegion`, `editor.timelineRegion`, `editor.toolsRegion` and `editor.exportRegion`, with the slots `preview`, `timeline`, `tools` and `export`. While a text is selected a fifth region, `editor.textRegion`, stands between the tools and the export, as "The text panel" says, and while the sound panel shows the region `editor.soundRegion` stands at the same place, as "The sound panel" says, and while a clip is selected the region `editor.clipRegion` stands there, directly after the place of the sound region, as "The clip panel" says. Core keeps any two of them from showing together. Root and regions are accessibility containers, so a region is found while it is empty and a control inside keeps its own identifier. On `options.showsInfo`, a seed start or a launch with `-drafts`, the shell also shows the text `editor.seedInfo`, the clips of the project and how many of its media files are in the media folder, the file of its music among them when it has one, as in "2 clips, 2 files", or "2 clips, 3 files" on `music-wide`. A file left under a temporary name by a failed write does not count. Under it, on `showsInfo` only, stand the two lines `editor.textInfo` and `editor.faceInfo` of "The text panel" and the line `editor.soundInfo` of "The sound panel". When the scene phase changes to one that is not active the shell calls `store.writeNow()` and starts `Task { await store.makeCover() }`, as "The project list" says.

### Saving a draft

Every project from the picker, and every seed on a launch with `-drafts`, is a draft on the shelf, and the store writes it as she edits.

- `EditorStore` holds `draft: OpenDraft?`, nil for a seeded start without `-drafts`, and `exportSettings`, both from its initialiser, which also takes the undo history a draft was written with (nil reads as an empty history) and makes the editing with `Editing(state:history:)`. The two slots `leave` and `export-choice` stand at its end, `leave` filled as "The project list" says and `export-choice` as "The choice kept with a draft" says.
- `write()` writes `Draft(project:exportSettings:changed:history:)` with `withoutBlankTexts` of the project, the store's export choice, the time of the write and `editing.history` through `draft.write`, whatever `unsaved` says, then sets the editing to `written()`. A throw is logged with `NSLog` and leaves `unsaved` as it was, so the next write tries again. With no draft nothing is written. `writeNow()` is `if editing.unsaved { write() }`, whatever the run.
- `send`, `undo` and `redo` call `write()` after the change when `editing.writesNow`, so a typed word or a gesture is written when its run ends, never at every report. The shell calls `writeNow()` when the app leaves the active phase. Every write is synchronous on the main actor.
- The Save button `editor.save` stands in the row of play after `editor.redo`: `Image(systemName: "square.and.arrow.down")` in a frame of 44 by 44 points, labelled by the word key `draft.save` (the slot `draft-saving` of the word files), always shown and enabled. A tap calls `store.write()` inside a transaction that disables animations.

### The preview

The preview, in the folder `App/Editor/Preview/`. It decides nothing and the slot `preview` of `EditorScreen` builds it as `PreviewPane(store: store)`.

- `App/Editor/Preview/PreviewPane.swift`: `PreviewPane`, the SwiftUI side. On top the picture area in the shape of `store.state.project.canvas`, its `width` to its `height`, as large as fits whole in the space above the button and in its middle. Under it one row: the button `editor.play`, which shows the pause symbol while `isPlaying` and the play symbol otherwise, is enabled by `canPlay`, sends `pause` or `play` and reads the key `preview.pause` or `preview.play`, and the label `editor.time` with `timeLabel`, then at its trailing end undo and redo, as "Undo beside play" says. The whole pane is fixed left to right, so nothing of it mirrors in Arabic.
- `App/Editor/Preview/PlayerPicture.swift` holds two types of one screen. `PlayerPicture` is a `UIViewRepresentable` that hands every state to the view. `PlayerPictureView` is a black UIKit view with the identifier `editor.picture` whose layer is the player's layer. It is made once with its player and takes the store, because it reads the state of the moment when the player calls it.
- The item: when the footage of `state.project` (its canvas, its clips, its media and its three values of sound) differs from the footage of the project the view was last handed, `renderPlan(for:)` and `makePlayerItem` build a new item from the store's media folder. A change of the texts alone builds none ("The preview and text" below), and neither does a change of the levels alone ("Levels while playing"). Builds are counted and the result of a build that a newer one superseded is dropped. Before the new item goes into the player the view gives it the playhead of that moment with the Engine's `seekExactly(to:)` of the item, so that time is the starting time of the item and the player makes it ready on that frame. The player is sent no seek for it. Before, the seek went to the player after the item was in, the player began the item at time zero and took the seek as a second order, and once, after a delete that left the playhead on a cut, the picture stayed on the clip before the cut: in that one run the player had begun to decode its frame at zero before the seek reached it, and the seek the view then sent at ready, to the same time, ended the first one as cancelled after that frame. Which of the two made the picture wrong is not known. Now neither happens after an edit. Until the status of the item is ready to play, the player follows nothing. Then the view reads the state of that moment. When its playhead is sought as the time the item was given, the view sends no seek: the player rests if the state is not playing and starts at once if it is. A seek to the time the player already has would bring no frame, because the player answers it at once with no work. When the playhead is another one, the view seeks to it with a completion handler, and when that seek has ended the player rests if the state is not playing and starts if it is. A seek that ended unfinished changes neither: the player then rests or starts from where it stands. An empty project (`canPlay` false) gets no item and no call of `makePlayerItem`, and the view lets go of the item of the last build. A build that throws leaves the player with no item, which is the black area.
- Between an edit and its result the area shows the picture from before the edit. While the new item is built the player keeps the item before it and stands still. Just before the new item goes into the player the view asks the layer for the picture it shows (`displayedReadOnlyPixelBuffer()`, which answers only while the player is paused, as it is after the stop every build starts with), makes an image of it and lays it over the layer in an image view that shows it whole and in its middle, as the layer shows a frame. The view observes the layer's `isReadyForDisplay` from before the item goes in, and takes the image away when the layer says the first frame of that item is ready. Each item put into the player is counted, and what was registered for an older item (its observation, its limit) does nothing. What is registered for the newest item reads the layer's flag of the moment, so a late report of ready for the item before, arriving just after a newer item went in, can take the image away early: the area is then black until the frame, as with no image, and never shows a wrong picture. Each time the view writes one line to the app's log with `NSLog`, which the simulator run keeps in `simulator-log.txt`: `old picture laid, item <number>`, or `old picture not laid, item <number>: the layer gave no buffer` (or `no image came of the buffer`) followed by `nothing lies` or `the one before still lies`, and `old picture taken away after <milliseconds> ms, item <number>:` followed by `the layer reported ready`, `the limit of 15 seconds`, `an empty project` or `a failed build`. The taking away writes a line only when an image lay. When the layer gives no picture (nothing was drawn yet, as at the opening of the editor) nothing is laid over it and the area is black until the first frame, and an image that already lies there stays. An empty project and a build that throws take the image away at once, so the area is black. An item that never gets a frame ready, such as one that fails, keeps the old picture for 15 seconds from the moment it went into the player, then the image goes and the area shows what the player shows. The image is drawn through Core Image with its own colour handling, which for a frame other than the plain colours of the seeds may differ a little from what the layer draws: not measured on a device. Whether the frame is on the screen in the same refresh in which the layer reports it ready is not measured either.
- Following the state: while it is not playing, a playhead other than the one the player was last sent to makes it seek, exact to the frame. A playhead at the very end of the project is sought as the last frame, the one that holds the last tick before the end: a text is drawn in a frame before its end, and sent to the time of the end itself the player showed that same last frame with no text in it. One seek is in flight at a time: a playhead that comes meanwhile is held, and when the seek in flight has landed the player is sent to the newest one if it differs. A seek that ends unfinished with no newer playhead waiting is forgotten, so the next state seeks again. When `isPlaying` turns true the player plays and starts to report as soon as it stands on the playhead: at once when no seek is in flight and either a seek with a completion handler has landed there before or the playhead is the starting time the item was given, otherwise when the seek to it has landed. So a start never begins while the player is on its way to another time. A start whose seek ended unfinished still begins, from where the player stands, and the reports give that time. While it is playing a change of the playhead never makes it seek, so its own reports do not. When `isPlaying` turns false it pauses, stops to report and seeks to the playhead.
- The layer asks for IOSurface buffers (`pixelBufferAttributes`). Without that request the simulator's layer refused every frame of a clip that filled the canvas unscaled and showed black, while a clip the composition scaled was shown. That was seen while the platform's own compositor drew the frames: the Engine's compositor now hands out every frame in a buffer with the IOSurface key, and whether the layer still needs its request is not measured. The request names a kind of buffer. What it costs on a device is not measured.
- Reporting: a time observer calls 30 times a second and sends `movePlayhead` with the player's time in ticks. It is removed the moment the view stops the player, and each call first reads `store.state.isPlaying`, so nothing is sent after a `pause`, also when the view has not been handed the paused state yet. When the item has played to its end the view sends `movePlayhead` with the project length and then `pause`, and the player then counts as standing on no playhead, so the paused state that follows seeks to the last frame as for any other playhead at the end.
- Sound plays through the player as it is. The app names no platform time type: the starting time of an item, the seeks and the time reports of this file go through `seekExactly(to:)` of the item and `seekExactly(to:then:)` and `reportTime(every:_:)` of the player, all of the Engine, in `Time`.

### The preview and text

How the preview follows a text, in `App/Editor/Preview/PlayerPicture.swift`. Every pixel of a text is drawn by the Engine's compositor from the render plan, and nothing is drawn above the picture area. No control that changes a text calls the preview: the view sees every state of the store, and any change of `project.texts` reaches the picture from there.

- When an item is built and when it is refreshed: a state whose project differs from the last one the view was handed is compared with it by `Project.sameFootage(as:)`. Another canvas, other clips or media (a framing alone aside), or another music file or start build a new item, as "The preview" says. Other levels alone give the standing item a new mix, as "Levels while playing" says. The same footage with other texts builds nothing: the view calls `refreshPicture(of:plan:media:)` with the plan of the new project on the standing item, which gives the item a new video composition and loads no file, and then follows the playhead and the playing of that state as for any other state. When `refreshPicture` throws, a new item is built, so the picture is never left with the texts from before. With the same footage and no standing item, which is an empty project or a build that failed, nothing is done.
- A build that lands late: a build takes time, and a text can change while it runs. While a build runs the view refreshes nothing, because the item in the player is the one of the footage before. When the build lands, the view reads the project of the store. When that project has the footage the build began with and other texts, the new item is refreshed once to it before it goes into the player, so its first frame has the texts of that moment. When that refresh throws, a new item is built from the project of the store.
- Which form is in use: a refresh alone. The view sets the new video composition and sends no seek and asks for no frame, and the paused player is expected to draw its frame again from the new video composition. The platform's page of `AVPlayerItem.videoComposition` does not say that it does, so the UI test of the typed letters holds it in the simulator run.
- The count of builds: on a seeded start, and only then, the accessibility value of `editor.picture` is the count of the items the view began to build, a whole number written with the digits 0 to 9. It is "0" before the first build, a build raises it by one, and a refresh and an empty project do not change it. With no seed the area has no value.

### Gestures on the picture

`App/Editor/Preview/TextGestures.swift` holds the gestures on the picture area. `TextGestures` is a view modifier with `let store: EditorStore`, which `PreviewPane` puts on the picture itself, after its shape and before the space around it, inside the pane that is fixed left to right. It adds four recognizers of UIKit through `UIGestureRecognizerRepresentable` and no view, layer, shape or drawing: nothing is drawn above the picture, and a selected text has no frame and no handle. The gestures act on the selected text wherever the finger lies on the picture. They decide nothing and hold no limit: each sends text actions, Core holds the values inside its limits, and the preview shows the result because it follows every text action ("The preview and text" above).

- A drag with one finger sends `text(.move)` with the centre of the text at the start of the drag plus `TextGeometry.canvasLength(ofPoints:pictureWidth:canvas:)` of the distance moved, for x and for y. The picture has the shape of the canvas, so its width gives both lengths. With two fingers on the picture nothing is moved.
- A pinch sends `text(.setStyle)` with the style at the start of the pinch and its size times the scale of the pinch.
- A twist sends `text(.turn)` with the rotation at the start of the twist plus the angle turned, in degrees, clockwise as she sees it. The sum can pass a whole turn: Core takes whole turns off.
- Every report of a recognizer sends a whole value worked out from the text as it was when the gesture began, which `GestureStart`, the coordinator of the gesture, keeps. None sends a difference to add. When a gesture begins with a text selected it sends `pause` before its first value. When it begins with no text selected a twist sends nothing at all, and a drag and a pinch act on a clip as "Punch in on the picture" says. A pinch and a twist are two recognizers that do not run together: two fingers do the one that is recognized first.
- A tap sends `text(.select)` for `text(nearestToX:y:at:)` of the tapped place in canvas pixels at the playhead, and nothing when that is nil. With the playhead at the very end of the project it asks at the last frame, the length less one frame, since that frame is the one shown. A tap sends no `pause`.
- The recognizers may sit on another view than the picture, so the file reads no view of a recognizer. The place of a tap and the distance of a drag come from the converter of the representable, in the space of the picture, and the width of the picture from its geometry (`onGeometryChange`), kept in `PictureWidth`.

### Punch in on the picture

While no text is selected and the playhead is inside the selected clip (`EditorState.framedByHand`), a pinch on the picture zooms that clip and a one finger drag moves it, in `App/Editor/Preview/TextGestures.swift`. They decide nothing: Core holds the zoom from 1 to 4 and the offset by the rule of the framing, as "Framing in the model and the plan" says.

- The coordinator of the drag and of the pinch is `ClipStart`, which holds the text's `GestureStart` beside the clip. Each report asks the text first, so with a text selected at the start the gesture acts on the text as before. When the gesture began with no text, the first report reads `framedByHand` and that clip once and sends `pause` when there is one. With no such clip the gesture sends nothing.
- A pinch sends `frame(.setZoom)` with the zoom at the start times the scale of the pinch. A drag sends `frame(.setOffset)` with the offset at the start plus the distance moved in shares of the picture: its width for x, and for y its width times the canvas height over its width, since the picture has the shape of the canvas. The pane is fixed left to right, so the picture never mirrors.
- Every value goes by `sendDragStep`, so the gesture is one drag run, and its end or a cancel calls `endDrag`: one step of undo and one write of the draft. A cancel sends no value. A twist does nothing to a clip.
- The picture follows each value with a refresh of the standing item, as for any change of framing, and builds nothing. When a level and a framing change in one state, `follow` refreshes the sound and then the picture.

- The row from left to right, 8 points between its parts so that all six fit the width of the phone: `editor.projects` of "The project list" in the slot `projects`, `editor.play`, `editor.time`, a spacer, then `editor.undo`, `editor.redo` and `editor.save`, the last described under "Saving a draft". Each of the two is a button with `Image(systemName:)` of `arrow.uturn.backward` and `arrow.uturn.forward` in a frame of 44 by 44 points, labelled by the word keys `editor.undo` and `editor.redo` (the slot `undo` of the word files).

- What one step is, and what an undo keeps of the selection, the playhead and the panels, is `Editing` of Core, as "Undo and redo" says. An undo of a change of the footage builds the item again, an undo of a text only refreshes the texts, with no line of `PlayerPicture.swift` changed.

### The timeline strip

The timeline strip, in the folder `App/Editor/Timeline/`. It decides nothing: places and times come from the scale of the strip, starts and positions from `Project`, and every change goes out as an `EditorAction`.

`App/Editor/Timeline/TimelineView.swift` holds two types of one screen. Neither is named `TimelineView`, because SwiftUI has a type of that name.

- `TimelineStrip` is the SwiftUI side, a `UIViewRepresentable` with `state: EditorState`, `mediaFolder: URL` and `send: @MainActor (EditorAction) -> Void`. The slot `timeline` of `EditorScreen` builds it from `store.state`, `store.mediaFolder` and `store.send` and gives it the height of `MusicBar.stripHeight(for:)`, 128 points while no text is selected and 104 while one is, because a wrapped UIKit view takes all the height it is offered.
- `TimelineStripView` is the UIKit view. `var state` is the state it draws, an `EditorState`, and setting it draws it. `var send` is the way to send an action. `let strip` is a `UIView` that holds the blocks and moves under the playhead: its left edge is time zero, so a point of it at x is the time `scale.time(atX: x)`, it is the clip row, 8 points below the top of the view and 56 points high, and the text row lies under it, as "Texts on the strip" says. Everything is drawn in `layoutSubviews()`: the blocks are made or removed to match the clips, `strip` is put with its left edge at the middle minus `scale.x(of: state.playhead)`, each block at `scale.x(of: project.start(of:))` with `scale.width(of: clip.duration)`, and the playhead line in the middle of the view above `strip`.
- A block is a container view in `strip` that holds its tiles ("Frames on the blocks") and, on top, one `UILabel` with the text "<position>\n<length as clockText>", the identifier `editor.clip.<position>`, the accessibility value `Project.clipInfo(_:)` of its clip, as "blue 0+1800 100 v", and the trait selected when its clip is the selection, which also gives it a wider outline. The label is the element, so a test reads a clip as before, and the container lets its touches through to `strip`. The playhead line has the identifier `editor.playhead` and the playhead's `clockText` as its value. Neither holds a word.
- A drag with one finger anywhere on the view is a scrub, unless it began as a press of half a second on a block, which moves the clip ("Moving a clip on the strip"): `pause` once when it starts, then `movePlayhead` with `scale.time(atX:)` of the playhead's x at the start minus the distance dragged to the right. Core clamps it. A tap inside `strip` sends `select` with the clip that `Project.clip(at:)` gives for the time under the finger. A tap anywhere else on the view (left of the first block, right of the last, in the 8 points above `strip` or anywhere below it, the empty part of the text row included) sends `clearSelection`. A tap on a text bar, on a handle or on the music bar never reaches this recognizer.
- The view never mirrors: every frame is set by hand from the left, and the view, `strip` and the blocks force left to right.

The slot `handles` is the two lines `// slot: handles` and `// end slot: handles` at the end of `layoutSubviews()` of `TimelineStripView`, after the blocks and the playhead have their frames. Code there runs inside the view on every draw, so it can read `state`, `send`, `strip`, `playhead` and `bounds`, and it cannot add a stored property. A view above the clips is added there when it is not there yet, and gets its frame there.

- A view that stays inside the bounds of `strip` is added to `strip`, in its coordinates. A new block is added above what `strip` already holds, so such a view is brought to the front there too.
- A view that can lie outside `strip` is added to the strip view itself, below `playhead` (`insertSubview(_:belowSubview:)`), at `strip.frame.minX` plus its x and `strip.frame.minY` plus its y. A trim handle is such a view: the start handle of the first clip and the end handle of the last clip are drawn outside the edges of `strip`, and UIKit never hands a touch outside a view's bounds to its subviews, so a handle inside `strip` would be drawn there and could not be dragged.
- The two recognizers of the strip view see the touches on a view above the clips too. A tap there also reaches the tap recognizer, which selects the clip at the finger's x when the point is inside `strip` and sends `clearSelection` otherwise, so a view above the clips whose tap must not select a clip or clear the selection has a tap recognizer of its own, as a trim handle has. A drag there can also start the scrub, so a view that is dragged has a pan recognizer of its own.
- To draw a state without sending it, as a trim drag does, set `state`. That preview is written over as soon as the store's state changes, because SwiftUI then hands the view the store's state again. So a drag that previews first sends `pause`, so that playback no longer moves the playhead under it, and then sets `state` on every step of the drag: a preview set in the same step as the `pause` can be written over once.

### The scale of the strip

How many points a second the strip shows. It is no part of the state of the editor: it is never stored with a draft, never in `EditorState` and never a step of undo.

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/TimelineGeometry.swift`: `TimelineScale`, Equatable and Sendable, with `pointsPerSecond` held between `TimelineScale.least`, 10, and `TimelineScale.most`, 240, by its initialiser. `TimelineScale.standard` is 60. `zoomed(by:)` gives the scale times a factor, held, and the scale unchanged for a factor that is not finite or not above 0. `x(of:)`, `time(atX:)` (rounded down to a frame, never below zero), `width(of:)` and `trimAmount(forDragOf:)` (whole frames, signed, rounded toward zero) work at its points a second. The static functions of `TimelineGeometry` are those of `standard`, unchanged.
- `TimelineStripView` holds `var scale: TimelineScale`, `.standard` when it is made. Each editor makes a strip view of its own, so every editor opens at 60 points a second. Every x, width and trim amount of `TimelineView.swift`, `TrimHandles.swift` and `TextBars.swift` reads it. The writing of a text bar is still placed by `TimelineGeometry.writing`, which takes points and no time.
- A `UIPinchGestureRecognizer` on the strip view keeps the scale it had when the pinch began and sets `scale` to that scale `zoomed(by:)` the recognizer's scale on every step, then lays the view out again. It sends no action: the playhead stays in the middle of the view on its time, and the strip is laid around it. The scrub takes one finger only, so a pinch never scrubs.
- Three slots follow: `recognizers` at the end of `init`, after the tap, which adds the press that moves a clip ("Moving a clip on the strip"), and `reorder` and `music-row` in `layoutSubviews()`, after the slot `handles` and before the text row. `reorder` draws the block of a running press lifted ("The lifted block"), and `music-row` lays out the music row ("The music row").

### Frames on the blocks

Each block shows frames of its clip along its length, as many square tiles as its width holds at the scale of the strip. Nothing of it is stored: the frames are made while an editor is open and kept in memory.

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Editor/BlockFrames.swift`: `BlockFrames`. `step` is 600 ticks. `times(of:)` gives the times a media's frames are made at: 0, `step` and on, below its duration, and 0 alone for a photo. `tileCount(width:tile:)` gives the whole tiles that cover a width, 0 for 0. `frameTime(of:media:tile:tile:scale:)` gives the frame a tile shows: the made time nearest the source time under the tile's left edge at the scale, never past the last made.
- `Sources/ReelsmithEngine/BlockFramesMaker.swift`: `makeBlockFrames(of:mediaFolder:)` makes a media's frames from its file at each of `times(of:)` by `AVAssetImageGenerator`, with a tolerance of half a step on both sides, the turn of the file applied and at most 168 pixels on the long side, as a `[Time: CGImage]`. A photo's still movie gives its one frame. It throws `sourceMissing` when the file is not in the folder and `cannotRead` when a frame cannot be made.
- `App/Editor/Timeline/FramesCache.swift`: `FramesCache`, one for each strip view (`TimelineStripView.frames`). The first ask for a media starts making its frames in a detached task, off the main actor, and gives nil until they come, then calls `changed`, which lays the strip view out again. A media is made once while the view lives, and a media whose frames cannot be made is not asked for again. Frames never go into `Media`. `FrameTiles`, in the same file, is the view of the tiles of one block: squares as high as the clip row (56 points) from its left end, each the frame of its time filling its square, cut at the end of the block.
- `TimelineStripView` gets `mediaFolder` from `TimelineStrip`. In `layoutSubviews()` each block's container is put at the block's place, its tiles and its label fill it, and the tiles get `tileCount` of the block's width and, for each, the frame of `frameTime` when it has come. A pinch only lays out again, so it makes nothing new.
- The tiles are the element `editor.frames.<position>` with the value "<tiles> <drawn> <made>": the tiles of the block, those that show a frame, and the media of the strip view whose frames have come. A test reads the frames through it and a clip through its block's label.
- Until its frames come a block is grey. The label is white with a dark shadow, so the position and the length stay readable on any frame.

### Moving a clip on the strip

The press and drag that moves a clip, in `App/Editor/Timeline/ClipPress.swift`. It decides nothing: the place a block is let go at is `Project.dropPosition(of:at:)` and the move is `reduce` of `moveClip`.

- `ClipPress` is a `UILongPressGestureRecognizer` with a minimum of 0.5 seconds on the strip view, added by `ClipPress.add(to:beside:)` from the slot `recognizers` with the scrub of the view. It is its own target and delegate, and the delegate of the scrub too.
- It takes only a touch whose view is the strip view or `strip` (a block lets its touches through to `strip`), so a press on a trim handle, a text bar or a handle of a bar never starts it, and it begins only when the point under the finger is inside `strip` on a clip, `Project.clip(at:)` of `scale.time(atX:)`. A press on empty strip moves nothing.
- At its start it sends `pause` and keeps the paused state of that moment, the pressed clip, the x of the left end of its block and the x of the finger. On every step it sets `state` of the strip view to `reduce` of the kept state with `moveClip(id, to: p)`, `p` being `dropPosition(of:at:)` of the kept project at the time of that left end moved by the finger since the start, at the scale of the strip view. The time under the finger itself is not used: a press held still on the right part of a block followed by a shorter one would then move it. Read from the left end, a press held still keeps its place, and a block moves when its left end passes the middle of a neighbour as the row stands with the block taken out. On release it sends that one action, so a move is one step of undo and one write of the draft. A cancelled press sets `state` back to the kept state and sends nothing.

With music it shows the text `sound.musicTitle` with the title of the music exactly as stored (content, never a key), the text `sound.musicLabel` with `state.musicLabel`, fixed left to right like the time of the preview, the same button `sound.addMusic` with the key `music.replace`, and the button `sound.removeMusic` with the key `music.remove`, which sends `sound(.removeMusic)`. A cancel calls nothing, so it changes nothing. A picked file goes to `addMusic`, and with music a new file replaces the music whole through `setMusic`.
- `addMusic(from source: URL, title: String?, to store: EditorStore) async -> MusicImport.Event`, on the main actor, in the same file, is the one way a file comes in, from the browser and from a video alike. It calls `importMusic` into `store.mediaFolder`, which copies the file, holds the right to read it while it copies and reads its facts, then sends `sound(.setMusic(file))` and answers `imported`. When `importMusic` throws it answers `refused(isProtected:)`, true for `protectedFile` alone, and throws nothing. The browser gives no title, so the title is the name of the file without its extension. The part copies and reads no file itself.
- The states of an import on the screen: the part holds one `MusicImport`, `idle` when it is made. A pick sends `picked`, then the event `addMusic` answers. While the state is not `idle` its `words` show as the text `sound.importWords`: `music.copying` while copying, `music.notReadable` or `music.protected` after a refusal. While copying the add buttons of the part are disabled, so one file comes in at a time, and so is the remove button, so a removal is not undone by the file that comes in. After a refusal the button `sound.importOk` with the key `music.ok` sends `failureConfirmed`, and a new pick is a way out too. The state lives in the part, so a part made anew starts `idle`, and a refusal that arrives while another part shows is not seen.
- The taps on `sound.removeMusic` and `sound.importOk` change the height of the part, so each makes its change inside a transaction that has `disablesAnimations` set, as the panel does.
- The part names no player and no item. The picture and the sound follow by the preview, since a change of the music builds a new item.
- Its seven word keys stand in the group `add-music` of the three word files: `music.add`, `music.replace`, `music.remove`, `music.copying`, `music.notReadable`, `music.protected` and `music.ok`.

### Music from a video

### The export button and its panel

The export button and its panel, in the folder `App/Editor/Export/`. They draw the `ExportState` of `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Export/`.

- `App/Editor/Export/ExportButton.swift`: `ExportButton`, the content of the slot `export` of `EditorScreen`. A row of two buttons, the choice `editor.exportSettings` ("The choice of size and rate") and then the export button with the identifier `editor.export` and the key `export.button`, enabled when `canStart` is true. It keeps the `ExportState` as its own state, since the store has no place for it. A tap sends `pause`, sends `started`, and runs `exportToPhotos` in a task with the project and the choice of that moment, the store's media folder and `options.exportFails`. The Engine reports progress off the main actor, so each value hops to the main actor before it is sent as `progress`. While the state is not `idle` a full screen cover with `ExportPanel` lies over the editor, so nothing behind it answers a touch.
- `App/Editor/Export/ExportPanel.swift`: `ExportPanel` draws the state: its words (`export.words`), the percent (`export.percent`, fixed left to right), the button `export.done` with the key `export.done` on `saved`, and the button `export.close` with the key `export.close` on `failed`. Both buttons send `closed`, which gives `idle` and takes the panel away. The editor state is not touched. While exporting there is no button, so there is no cancel.
- `App/Editor/Export/ExportToPhotos.swift`: `exportToPhotos(project:settings:mediaFolder:fails:progress:)` makes the plan with `renderPlan(for:settings:)`, calls `export(plan:media:mediaFolder:to:progress:)` with the media of the project and the output place `<temporary folder>/<uuid>.mp4`, asks the photo library for the right to add only (`PHPhotoLibrary.requestAuthorization(for: .addOnly)`), and adds the file as a video (`PHAssetCreationRequest`). It reads `readVideoFacts` of the written file before the save, nil when that read throws. It returns the event that ends the export, with those facts: `saved`, `failed(notAllowed: true)` when the right to add is not given, and `failed(notAllowed: false)` for every error of the plan, of the Engine and of the save. The file is removed after the save and after a failure. With `-export-fails` the output place is `<temporary folder>/<uuid>/export.mp4` in a folder that is not there, so the Engine throws `cannotWrite` before any work and reports no progress.

### The awake screen

The phone does not lock while an export runs, since an export longer than the auto lock would fail.

- `Sources/ReelsmithCore/Export/ExportState.swift`: `keepsScreenAwake` is true for `exporting(percent:)`, whatever the percent, and false for `idle`, `saved` and `failed(notAllowed:)`.
- `App/Editor/Export/ExportButton.swift`: one `onChange(of: exportState)` sets `UIApplication.shared.isIdleTimerDisabled` to `exportState.keepsScreenAwake`, so the idle timer is off from the tap on Export to the saved or the failed panel. No other file of the app names the idle timer, and the Engine names none.

### The choice kept with a draft

The choice of size and rate belongs to the draft it was made on and comes back with it.

- `App/Editor/EditorStore.swift`: `exportSettings` is the store's, given by its initialiser, so a store opened on a stored draft can start from the draft's `exportSettings`. A new project and every seed start at `ExportSettings.startChoice`. `choose(_:)` sets it and then calls `write()`, never `writeNow()`: a choice is no part of the undo history and leaves `unsaved` false, so `writeNow()` would write nothing. With no draft, as on a seeded start without `-drafts`, nothing is written. A write that throws leaves the label as chosen, is logged, and is tried again at the next write.
- `App/Editor/Export/ExportButton.swift`: the label reads `store.exportSettings`, the sheet is given it, its `choose` calls `store.choose`, and the export runs at `store.exportSettings` as it is at the tap. An undo or a redo leaves the choice as it is.
